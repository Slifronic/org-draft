05 · callout
Why the form is not optional
A blank answer does not make you neutral
The draft balances four things at once: team size, which division you lean toward, how long you have been in the chapter, and whether you are interested in leading.
Every one of those comes from the form. Leave it blank and you are not neutral — you are a coin flip.

09 · three
The draft
Not random, and not a popularity contest
Size
Orgs come out within one member of each other.
Division
Whichever tracks the chapter runs get spread evenly rather than clustering.
Tenure
First-years and returning members are mixed, so no Org is all new.
Plus leadership interest, and — if the chapter uses them — personality types. That last one is what the balance score measures. Lower is better.
10 · callout
The flexible pieces
“Either / no preference” is a real answer
Members with no division preference are what let a lopsided chapter still come out even. They go wherever the numbers need them.
If you genuinely do not mind, saying so is more useful than guessing.

12 · three
The Org Cup
Three bonuses sit on top
Cross-division
Turn up on the other side of the chapter and earn extra. This exists to stop two divisions becoming two clubs.
No preference
Members with no division preference earn a little extra for exploring.
Full team
If most of an Org shows up to the same thing, the whole Org scores. Dragging your friends along is the point.
Standings rank on points per member, not raw totals — otherwise an Org with eleven people beats one with nine regardless of effort.

15 · callout
The one thing that goes wrong
The browser copy is not a backup
It disappears if the cache is cleared or the officer changes laptops.
Export a save file at the end of each semester. Never let one person’s browser hold the only copy of anything.
