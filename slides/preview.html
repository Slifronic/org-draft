What an Org is
A cross-section of the chapter, not a friend group

An Org is a small standing team inside the chapter. You did not pick it and neither did your friends — it was dealt so that every Org looks roughly like the chapter as a whole.
That is the point. You end the year knowing ten people you would not otherwise have met, across both divisions and both year groups.
04 · three
Why you did not choose
What the draft balanced when it placed you
Size
Every Org comes out within one member of every other, so no team is carrying a numbers disadvantage in the Cup.
Division
Consulting and case competition people are spread across all the Orgs rather than clustering into two camps.
Tenure
First-years and returning members are mixed, so no Org is all new and none is a closed circle of veterans.
A fourth axis spreads members who said they are interested in leading, so every Org has somebody willing to organise it. If the chapter uses personality types, a fifth keeps any one Org from being nine of the same person.

06 · callout
Why the form is not optional
A blank answer does not make you neutral
Every axis the draft balances comes from your form answers. Leave one blank and the sorter has nothing to work with.
You are not neutral — you are a coin flip, and so is the Org that ends up with you.
07 · cupstruct
The Org Cup
How the competition is put together
The season
Roughly fourteen weeks. Every Org starts at zero in week one.
The unit
One member, at one thing, on one date. That is what gets logged.
The total
Every point any member earns is credited to their Org, plus whole-team bonuses.
The ranking
Orgs are ranked on points per member, not on raw totals.
The winner
Whoever is top of that table when the season ends.
08 · kindlist
Earning points · 1 of 2
Six kinds of thing are worth points
Meetings
General chapter meetings, division workshops, study and prep sessions. Individually the smallest amount — but they happen most weeks, so over a semester they are the largest share of most members’ totals.
Competition
Case competitions, conference events, state and national qualifiers. The highest per-event value in the catalog, because it is the chapter competing in public. Usually scored twice: once for entering, again for placing.
Service
Community service, philanthropy, volunteering hours. Often logged by the hour rather than the event, so a long Saturday counts more than a short one.
09 · kindlist
Earning points · 2 of 2
And the three that are easy to forget
Social
Mixers, banquets, retreats, Org socials. Deliberately worth less than competition — but these are what turn ten people who were assigned to each other into a team that shows up for each other.
Leadership
Running a workshop, leading a session, holding a role, mentoring a first-year. Worth a lot per instance because few people do it. This is the category most within your control.
Recruiting
Bringing a guest, working a tabling shift, getting someone to actually join. Scored generously, since a chapter that does not recruit has no next year.

11 · three
Earning points
Three bonuses, and how to trigger them
Cross-division
Go to something on the other side of the chapter. Every time. This bonus exists specifically to stop consulting and case competitions becoming two separate clubs.
No preference
If you told the form you had no division preference, you pick up extra on anything you attend. You are being paid to explore.
Full team
If most of your Org turns up to the same thing, the whole Org scores a bonus on top of the individual points. Bring your Org.
The third one is the only bonus you cannot earn alone, and it is the largest. Dragging four people to a workshop is worth more than going to two on your own.
12 · callout
Why the leaderboard looks like that
Ranked per member, not by total
An Org of eleven would beat an Org of nine on raw totals every time, whatever either of them actually did.
Dividing by headcount means the Cup measures participation. A small Org that all turns up beats a large one that half does.
