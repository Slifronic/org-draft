What the sign-up form has to ask
  1. Name — full name, one per member. Required.
  2. Email — for the Lead to reach them. Not used by the draft; leave it off any published tab.
  3. Year in the club — first year or second-and-up. Ask about club tenure, not academic standing: a junior who just joined drafts as a first-year. Required.
  4. Which division they lean toward — one option per track your club runs, plus an either / no preference option. Keep that last option: undecided members are the flex pieces that let the sort even out lopsided groups. Set your own track names under Club setup above. Required if your club uses tracks.
  5. MBTI type — four letters. Only if you switched it on under Club setup. If you do use it, make it a required question with a link to one specific free test and a deadline: a partial column produces a type read that describes only the people who bothered to answer. Plenty of clubs should leave this off entirely.
  6. Leadership interest — yes/no, for longer-standing members. The draft spreads volunteers so every team gets its share of leaders.
  7. Anything the draft should know — optional free text. Members with a note get flagged so you can hand-adjust.

On making MBTI required

Requiring it is a real tradeoff and worth naming out loud. You get a complete read and an Assistant that can actually recommend changes; you also make members hand over a personality result to a club, and some will find a test online, click through it in ninety seconds, and give you noise. Two things make it hold up: tell members plainly what it's used for (spreading types across Orgs, not judging anyone), and link one specific free test so everyone is answering the same instrument. If someone genuinely refuses, take the type they'd guess rather than leaving the field empty — a guess is closer to true than a blank.

The tool reads raw Google Form exports and matches the columns itself. You don't need to reformat anything.

Connecting your Google Sheet

This runs entirely in your browser — there's no server. To read a sheet directly, it has to be readable without a login.

Option A — Publish just the responses (recommended)

  1. In the Form's response sheet, make a new tab with only the columns the draft needs: name, year, track, MBTI, lead interest, notes. Leave emails out.
  2. File → Share → Publish to web, choose that tab, format Comma-separated values (.csv), Publish.
  3. Paste the link it gives you below.
  4. Unpublish after the draft — File → Share → Publish to web → Stop publishing.

Option B — Link sharing

Set the sheet to Anyone with the link → Viewer and paste the normal URL. Same privacy tradeoff, less setup.

The privacy call

Publishing a tab of ~100 classmates' names and personality types makes it readable by anyone with the link. Names and years are low-risk; MBTI results and emails are more personal than they look. Keep contact details off the published tab, unpublish when you're done, and if you'd rather publish nothing, use CSV upload — the file never leaves your computer.

Who can do what

Three roles. Member sees standings, their own Org page, and their own points — nothing else, and no private notes. Admin runs the club: draft, attendance, points, actions, manual moves. Master is you: everything an admin can do, plus this page.

Anyone who signs in with a Google account that is not on this list is a member. Add an officer by their exact Google address — the one they actually sign in with.

Clubs on this system

Each club is its own world: separate roster, separate teams, separate points, separate officers. Nothing crosses between them. Somebody signing up picks their club from a list and has to produce its join code, so the club's name being public does not let anyone in.

Give the join code out the way you would a door code — to the people who belong there. If it gets around, Edit the club below to issue a new one. Clear data empties a club’s roster, points and attendance for a new semester while keeping its accounts and setup; Delete removes the club and everything in it.

Password accounts

For members who cannot use a Google account. These are weaker than Google sign-in — a password can be shared, guessed, or reused from somewhere else — so hand them out only where they are needed, and give each person their own rather than one shared login.

There is no self-signup: an account exists only because you created it here. Set a starting password, tell the person, and have them change it under their own name once they are in.

What this actually protects

Hiding a button is not security, so the page does not rely on it. Every write carries the signed token Google issued to whoever is holding the page, and the Apps Script verifies that signature and re-checks the role before it touches the sheet. A member who edits the page in their browser can rearrange what they see; the shared sheet will still refuse them.

Your password

You are signed in with a username, so you can change your own password here. It takes effect immediately; other devices stay signed in until their session expires.
