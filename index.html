The club’s sign-up form

The sign-up form's questions

Edit the club's own form here. Changes go straight to the live form — anyone who opens the link next sees them.

What the sign-up form has to ask
  1. Name — full name, one per member. Required.
  2. Email — leave it off any published tab.
  3. Year — 1st year or 2nd year. Required. This is the only thing shown next to a name; there are no titles.
  4. Track — Consulting, Case competition (FLC) or Polaris, plus not sure yet. Keep that last option: undecided members are what let the sort even out lopsided groups.
  5. MBTI type — four letters. Only if you switched it on under Club setup. Make it required and link one specific free test, or leave it off entirely.
  6. Availability — when they are usually free. Shown to officers next to the name when forming teams.
  7. Anything the draft should know — optional free text. Members with a note get flagged.

Raw Google Form exports work as they are. Nothing needs reformatting.

Connecting your Google Sheet

This runs in your browser. To read a sheet directly, it has to be readable without a login.

Option A — publish just the responses

  1. In the response sheet, make a tab with only what the draft needs: name, year, track, MBTI, availability, notes. Leave emails out.
  2. File → Share → Publish to web, choose that tab, format CSV, Publish.
  3. Paste the link below.
  4. Unpublish when the draft is done.

Option B — link sharing

Set the sheet to Anyone with the link → Viewer and paste the normal URL.

Publishing a tab makes it readable by anyone with the link. Keep contact details off it, and unpublish when you are done — or use CSV upload, where the file never leaves your computer.

Org Cups

An Org earns a cup every time at least 80% of its members hand an assignment in on time, and EBOD awards cups for things like bootcamp turnout. There is no leaderboard: cups are something an Org collects together, not a ranking. Your own commitment points are a private record on your Profile.

Member Drive folders

What this actually protects

Hiding a button is not security, so the page does not rely on it. Every write carries the signed token Google issued to whoever is holding the page, and the Apps Script verifies that signature and re-checks the role before it touches the sheet. A member who edits the page in their browser can rearrange what they see; the shared sheet will still refuse them.
